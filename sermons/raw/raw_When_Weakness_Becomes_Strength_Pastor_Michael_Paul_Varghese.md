# When Weakness Becomes Strength

**Speaker:** Pastor Michael Paul Varghese  
**Date:** 23 August 2026  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pPC_R161_Q4

## Cleaned Transcription

The message begins with thanksgiving and worship. The congregation is directed to remember the good things God has done and to give thanks for salvation, the new birth, God's mercy, and His continuing goodness. The worship focuses on waiting on the Lord, trusting Him as the everlasting God, and refusing to be ashamed of the gospel of Jesus Christ.

The teaching then turns to confidence in God's goodness. The speaker explains from [Psalm 105](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/PSA.105.KJV) that God's people should remember His works and keep His deeds in their hearts. Thanksgiving is presented as a way of keeping God's faithfulness before us.

The central teaching is drawn from [2 Corinthians 12:9-10](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/2CO.12.9-10.KJV): God's grace is sufficient, and His strength is made perfect in weakness. Paul learned to regard weakness differently. Weakness is not the final statement about a believer. It can become the place where God's grace and ability are most clearly seen.

The speaker defines grace as God's ability supplied where human ability is insufficient. This means that when a person reaches a point where strength, wisdom, qualification, or natural ability seems to end, God's grace is still available. The response is not despair but faith: bring the weakness to God and expect His strength to work there.

The message then connects this truth with [Lamentations 3:22-24](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/LAM.3.22-24.KJV). God's mercies do not fail. They are new every morning. The Lord is our portion, so our hope can rest in Him. The speaker emphasizes that we can speak to our own souls and remind ourselves that God's mercy and faithfulness, rather than our performance, are the basis of our hope.

From [1 Corinthians 10:13](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/1CO.10.13.KJV), the speaker explains that temptation and trial are not beyond God's provision. God is faithful and provides a way of escape. From [James 1:5-6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/JAS.1.5-6.KJV), the teaching is that wisdom can be requested from God in faith. When a person does not know what to do or say, that lack can become an opportunity to receive God's wisdom rather than to remain double-minded.

The speaker returns to the promise in [Hebrews 13:5-6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.13.5-6.KJV): God will never leave or forsake His people. Because of this promise, the correct response is boldness: "The Lord is my helper. I will not fear what man shall do unto me." Confidence is grounded in God's unchanging character, not in self-confidence.

The message develops this idea through [2 Corinthians 1:3](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/2CO.1.3.KJV), where God is described as the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort. God's mercy is not a minor aspect of His character. It is presented as central to the gospel. The cross is used as the clearest expression of that mercy: Jesus suffered and offered forgiveness to those who did not understand what they were doing.

The speaker then uses [Isaiah 52:14-15](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/ISA.52.14-15.KJV) to emphasize the depth of Christ's suffering and connects it to the mercy released to the nations. The application is that believers should not live by performance, qualification, or personal strength. They should live by receiving God's grace and mercy.

[Psalm 145:8-9](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/PSA.145.8-9.KJV) is used to reinforce the same point: the Lord is gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, rich in mercy, and good to all. God's works are to be viewed through the lens of His goodness and mercy.

The speaker then turns to [Mark 1:40-42](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/MRK.1.40-42.KJV). A man with leprosy asked Jesus whether He was willing to make him clean. Jesus responded with compassion and healed him. The point is that the question was not whether Jesus had power, but whether He was willing. Compassion revealed God's willingness to help.

The message also references [Micah 7:18](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/MIC.7.18.KJV), presenting God as One who delights in mercy. The repeated call is to let this truth move from information into the inward life until it changes how a believer responds to pressure, weakness, failure, temptation, and uncertainty.

The speaker returns again to Paul's example in [2 Corinthians 12:9-10](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/2CO.12.9-10.KJV). The believer can choose to trust God's supply rather than personal capacity. What looks like disadvantage can become the place where God's strength is revealed.

The message then turns to [Hebrews 2:17-18](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.2.17-18.KJV). Jesus became like His brothers and sisters so that He could be a merciful and faithful High Priest. Because He suffered and was tested, He is able to help those who are tested. The speaker presents this as a practical comfort: Jesus understands human struggle and ministers mercy and help.

The sermon closes by returning to the themes of mercy, grace, strength, and God's faithfulness. The congregation is encouraged to receive these truths deeply, to remember that God's grace is sufficient, to refuse fear, and to expect God's provision in difficult circumstances. [Psalm 91](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/PSA.91.KJV) is also declared as a confession of God's protection and faithfulness.

The final exhortation is to remember that God's goodness is not earned by perfect performance. His mercy is our portion. When we are weak, we can look to Him and expect His grace. The speaker closes with thanksgiving and encourages the church to carry these truths into daily life.
