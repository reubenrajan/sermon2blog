# The Truth Is Not Out There

*Study Blog Edition*

**Speaker:** Pastor Michael Paul Varghese

## The Great Shepherd of the Sheep

Praise the Lord Jesus. We are here again by His grace for another Wednesday of meditation in the Word of God, our weekly Bible study. We worship Him who is worthy. We give Him thanks and praise: the invisible God, the Holy One of Israel. We worship the Father and give thanks for the Holy Spirit who lives in us. We ask Him to unveil Jesus to us.

We are grateful that Jesus is at the right hand of the Majesty on high, pleading and interceding for us. We are grateful for the privilege of taking our place as His body on the earth. To Him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing, glory, honor, and power forever.

Hebrews 13:20-21 gives us a prayer that says, in part, that the God of peace brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, and that He would make us mature in every good work to do His will, working in us what is well pleasing in His sight through Jesus Christ.

There is a wonderful picture here. Jesus was the Lamb led to the slaughter. He was silent before His shearers. He laid down His life for the sheep. After His resurrection, He became the great Shepherd of the sheep. Through the covenant blood that He Himself shed, He is now committed to perfect that which concerns us.

He is doing His part, pleading for us and working in us what is well pleasing in His sight. All of this is in Christ, who has given us the privilege of being in Him. To Him be glory and honor forever and ever.

Some things are eternal and some things are temporary. Jesus has passed into the eternal realm, and He can faithfully undertake for us, watchfully and prayerfully keeping us. We look to His goodness and mercy.

## Truth Has More Than One Side

Every now and then, we look for Scriptures that minister peace and quietness to our souls. John 3:16 is one of those Scriptures: God loved the world and gave His only begotten Son so that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life. It is filled with goodness and mercy.

But there are other verses that are true even though they are not as comfortable to hear. John 3:35-36 says that the Father loves the Son and has given all things into His hand. Whoever believes in the Son has everlasting life, but whoever does not believe the Son shall not see life, and the wrath of God remains on him.

That is not a verse that people naturally want to display everywhere. Yet it is true. There are different sides to the multifaceted wisdom of God. He is the only wise God, and some things about Him are simply the way He is.

There is a strong drive toward universal acceptance in a sinful world. We should appeal to people by every means possible through the precious blood of Jesus. Yet there is also a serious truth: if people do not choose the mercy of God in this life, they will face consequences. This is the best time to accept the wonderful mercy of God.

Jesus is both the Lamb and our propitiation. The Lamb is gentle, kind, merciful, and precious. He takes away the sin of the world. Propitiation means that the wrath of God against sin was satisfied through the sacrifice of Jesus. God hates sin and everything that is evil. Jesus came and bore the judgment that sin deserved.

If a person does not accept the mercy offered in the Lamb's blood, the wrath that Jesus bore remains something that person must face. Jesus became the propitiation, the satisfactory sacrifice, and He bore the wrath of God against sin.

These truths must be received together. We cannot understand the compassionate Lamb while rejecting the holiness of God. We need both sides or we will paint the wrong picture of the One we are dealing with.

## Jesus Our Advocate and Propitiation

First John 2:1-2 says that John writes to believers so that they do not sin. Yet, if anyone does sin, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. He is our Advocate with the Father.

An advocate is someone called to defend you. In the heavenly court of God, the righteous Judge, we have someone to call upon who will defend us. Every believer has Jesus Christ the righteous as an Advocate.

John then says that Jesus is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world. The word translated propitiation is connected with the idea of the mercy seat. In the Old Testament tabernacle, the blood of the sacrifice was applied at the mercy seat. The sacrifice dealt with sin before God.

Today Jesus becomes our mercy seat, and His blood is our mercy. He is the appropriate sacrifice, the sacrifice that satisfies the holiness and justice of God.

This sacrifice was not only for believers. Jesus offered Himself for the sins of the whole world. Believers have accepted what He has done. The rest of the world is still being called to receive it. Jesus has provided the sacrifice, but people must respond to the mercy offered to them.

Therefore, today is the day and now is the hour to take advantage of what this precious Lamb has done. We have an Advocate who pleads for us and speaks on our behalf with the Father, the righteous Judge of the whole earth.

## Love That Begins With God

First John 4:10 says that love is not first defined by our love for God. It says that He loved us and sent His Son to be the propitiation for our sins. The sacrifice was offered first. We did not know about it or earn it. He first loved us and gave Himself for us.

The world has little idea about this. We owe it to people to explain and offer this wonderful mercy to them.

The next verse says that because God so loved us, we ought also to love one another. God's love now affects how we walk on this earth.

Psalm 23 speaks about walking through the valley of the shadow of death. It is not only death itself, but the shadow of death. There are thoughts and ideas that come against the mind and imagination. Fear can enter the mind even when the feared thing is not the reality.

A shadow is not the reality. The reality is that we have the life of God. The reality is that God loves us. The reality is that Jesus is pleading for us. The reality is that we share His life. Everything else is a shadow.

Yet shadows can still interfere with clear thinking. Therefore, we must make an effort to think on what is true. We have to learn to think life in the midst of shadows of death all around us.

## The Revelation of Jesus Christ

John also wrote the book of Revelation. Revelation 1:1 calls it the revelation of Jesus Christ. It is not merely the revelation of John. God gave the revelation to Jesus Christ to show His servants the things that must shortly come to pass, and Jesus signified it through His angel to His servant John.

Revelation 1:3 says, "Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein: for the time is at hand."

If the time was described as being at hand two thousand years ago, then we should recognize how close the fulfillment appears to be now. There is a blessing attached to reading, hearing, and keeping these words.

Reading Scripture should not be treated as a private intellectual exercise only. In the original setting, reading often meant proclaiming the words aloud to a gathered community. We should give the Word a voice. Read it and say it.

As we continue through Revelation, we notice that one of the main figures is the Lamb. The Lamb is repeatedly magnified in the book. Jesus is presented through the character of the Lamb: gentle, merciful, innocent, and lovable.

Then Revelation also shows the Lamb in another aspect. The church is pictured as having been gathered to Christ, and the marriage of the Lamb becomes a central image. Revelation 19 speaks of the marriage of the Lamb and says that His wife has made herself ready. The fine linen represents the righteous deeds of the saints.

There is an amazing picture here for our imagination. The same Lamb who is gentle and merciful is also the holy Judge of sin.

## The Wrath of the Lamb

Revelation 6:12-17 describes the opening of the sixth seal. John sees a great earthquake, the sun becoming black as sackcloth, the moon becoming like blood, stars falling to the earth, the heavens departing as a scroll, and mountains and islands being moved out of their places.

John is recording what he saw. He is not simply adding his own interpretation. He reports the events as he witnessed them.

Then the kings of the earth, great men, rich men, chief captains, mighty men, bondmen, and free men hide themselves in the dens and rocks of the mountains. They say to the mountains and rocks, "Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb."

Notice the words: the wrath of the Lamb.

The Lamb is not only tender. The Lamb also has wrath. Revelation 6:17 says that the great day of His wrath has come, and asks who shall be able to stand.

These are not abstract future ideas that have no connection with human choice. The message is that people need to respond while mercy is available. We should not minister these truths with a desire to condemn anyone. We should set forth the truth.

There is a reality of judgment. There is also a present opportunity for mercy. Jesus has taken the wrath for those who receive Him. We should therefore offer the Gospel of reconciliation without fear.

## Do Not Be Uncomfortable With Truth

We should not become uncomfortable when we hear truth. We should make adjustments. We may squirm when the truth confronts us, but we need a steady diet of truth because we are constantly bombarded by lies and shadows of fear and death.

The church should be one place where truth is available. If someone wants to hear the truth, it should be available in the house of God.

We may have listened to lies, absorbed fear, or spent too much time looking at the shadows around us. The answer is not to avoid truth. The answer is to return to truth.

As we read and study the book of Revelation, we can do so with confidence in Christ. We are not reading it merely to satisfy curiosity. We are being reminded of the seriousness of the choices people make and the mercy that is available now.

We should be grateful for the grace of God and for the privilege of knowing that the Lamb has offered Himself. Our response should be to stand on the right side of this truth and continue to offer God's mercy to the world.

## Wisdom Is the Principal Thing

Proverbs 4:7 says, "Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding."

It continues by saying that when wisdom is embraced, it brings honor. This aligns with Proverbs 3, where we are told not to trust in the Lord with part of our heart, but with all our heart. We are also told not to lean on our own understanding.

God's wisdom teaches us how to make the right choices. His correction is part of His love. Wisdom is described as more valuable than silver, gold, or anything else that can be desired.

The people who seem rich and powerful but reject the mercy of the Lamb cannot finally escape the consequences of rejecting God's wisdom. We are warned in advance, so we have the ability to choose now in view of the future.

Wisdom is described as more precious than rubies. Length of days is in her right hand, and in her left hand are riches and honor. She is a tree of life to those who hold her.

What do we need first? Not merely riches, honor, or long life. We need wisdom. These other things are presented as consequences of walking in wisdom.

Jesus is the embodiment of that wisdom. He is the wisdom of God. When we understand Him, these other things can become our portion in the right way. We can live from a place of rest rather than constantly chasing the things the world tells us to chase.

## The Wisdom of Humility

James 3 addresses the tongue and the inconsistency of blessing and cursing from the same mouth. A fountain cannot produce both sweet water and bitter water. A fig tree cannot produce olives, and a vine cannot produce figs.

The point is that our speech should reveal the nature of the wisdom by which we live.

We should choose to be a blessing. Our tongues should produce what strengthens and builds others up. We should separate our speech from bitterness and use it for things that are fruitful and helpful.

James 3:13 asks who is wise and endued with knowledge and says that such a person should show by good conduct and works the meekness of wisdom.

The wisdom of God is humble. The world has another kind of wisdom that is driven by pride, competition, striving, envy, and self-promotion.

There is always a temptation to be proud and to make ourselves the center. The wisdom of God is the opposite. It is a humble kind of wisdom. We choose the low road rather than insisting that we must be the highest person in the room.

This can be difficult because the world keeps reminding us that being low means being insignificant. Yet we are in good company when we walk humbly because that is how our King walked.

## Meekness Is Not Weakness

Matthew 11:25-30 shows Jesus thanking the Father because certain things are hidden from the wise and prudent in the world's sense and revealed to those who come as babes.

This does not mean that God opposes true wisdom. It means that worldly wisdom, worldly status, and worldly pride do not give a person access to the things of God.

Jesus then says that all things are delivered to Him by the Father and that no one knows the Father except the Son and those to whom the Son reveals Him. Therefore, we should interpret the Father through the Son. Jesus unveils the Father to us.

Then Jesus gives the invitation: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

There is a progression here. The burdened sinner comes to Jesus and finds rest. Then the disciple takes the yoke of Jesus and learns from Him. Jesus says that He is meek and lowly in heart.

That is where true wisdom is found: in meekness and lowliness.

Meekness is not weakness. It is power under control. It is strength governed by the right master. We are learning to bring our hearts under His control rather than simply doing whatever our own impulses demand.

The result is rest for the soul. The soul must be trained to find rest in what Jesus calls good instead of waiting for worldly conditions to become perfect before it can rest.

We are different because we have decided that if this is what Jesus wants, then this is what we want. His yoke is easy and His burden is light.

Therefore, our targets cannot be the same as the world's targets. We want the rest that comes from choosing what Jesus calls good.

## Get Wisdom, Not More Fear

Proverbs 3 and James 3 point us toward the same conclusion. We need wisdom, not a constant obsession with spiritual threats.

The Scripture says that the wise person is humble. Wisdom produces the ability to respect authority, honor those whom God says to honor, and live with the right priorities.

It does not make sense to think according to the world and then expect God's wisdom to function the same way. God's wisdom is hidden from pride and revealed to those who are willing to be lowly and teachable.

A basic question for self-examination is this: Have I been having a difficult time with humility?

All of us face that temptation. There is a part of human nature that wants to be the head on its own. Genesis shows this desire when people wanted to make a name for themselves. God later told Abraham that He would make Abraham's name great.

The difference is crucial. We should not force our own greatness. We should let God establish what He wants, in His timing and through His process.

That may require waiting. It may be difficult for the flesh. Yet God's Word will come to pass. He cannot forget what He has said. He remembers His covenant, His Word, and His promises.

## Two Kinds of Wisdom

James 3:14-18 gives a direct contrast between earthly wisdom and wisdom from above.

Where there is bitter envy and strife in the heart, we are told not to boast and not to lie against the truth. This kind of wisdom does not come from above. It is earthly, sensual, and devilish.

Where envy and strife exist, there is confusion and every evil work.

By contrast, the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be entreated. It is full of mercy and good fruits. It is without partiality and without hypocrisy.

This gives us a practical picture of wisdom.

Are we pure in what we are thinking about? Are we peaceable? Are we gentle? Are we easy to live with? Are we full of mercy? Are we producing good fruit? Are we free from partiality and hypocrisy?

The target is to be like Jesus. People may disappoint us or let us down, but that does not change the target. We can still follow Jesus.

Wisdom is not merely asking God for a shortcut in a difficult situation. Wisdom means learning to think like Him and becoming like Him.

## Mercy Is the Knowledge of God

Hosea 6:6 says, "I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings."

Mercy is connected with the knowledge of God. Jesus used this Scripture Himself and told people to go and learn what it means.

Matthew 9:13 says, "Go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice."

Jesus was teaching people to study the Bible. He was not asking for religious activity without understanding. He wanted people to learn the meaning of the Scriptures.

Imagine Jesus saying to us, "Go and find out what this means. Go study these things. Learn these things."

That is why Bible study matters. People may say, "You are always talking about the Bible. You are always studying the Bible." The answer is that Jesus wants us to know what His Word means.

We can study physics, chemistry, biology, and many other subjects. Those studies have their place. But we must not forget to study the Word of God and understand its meaning.

Jesus is the Word made flesh. To know His Word is to learn His ways and understand His heart.

## What Does the Lord Require?

Micah 6:8 gives another clear statement: God has shown us what is good and what He requires. We are to do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with our God.

Notice the connection.

We are to be full of mercy. We are to love mercy. We are to walk humbly. The meekness of wisdom is not a separate idea. These truths belong together.

We can examine our lives and ask whether we have been affected by a desire to be high and mighty, force our way through, or become self-made people.

Instead, we should let God lift up our heads. We should let Him establish His goodness in His work.

## Paul Serves With Humility

Acts 20 gives us a real example in the life of the apostle Paul. Paul was coming toward the end of his ministry and was on his way to Jerusalem.

Earlier, in Troas, a young man named Eutychus fell from the third story while Paul was preaching late into the night. Paul went down, embraced him, and the young man was found alive.

After that, Paul traveled and eventually came to Miletus, where he sent for the elders of the church at Ephesus. He wanted to speak to the church leaders.

There was an order to this. Paul did not simply address everyone in an undefined way. He called the elders, the pastors, those who had oversight.

Acts 20:18-20 begins Paul's farewell message. He reminds them how he had been with them through all seasons and says that he served the Lord with all humility of mind, with many tears and temptations.

Life is full of seasons. There are good seasons, difficult seasons, happy seasons, sad seasons, and challenging seasons. Paul says that in all those seasons he served the Lord with humility.

Some experiences are painful. We learn the true nature of people through different seasons, and those discoveries can hurt. Yet Paul kept serving in humility.

He did not hold back anything that was profitable to the people. He taught publicly and from house to house. He continued to give what would benefit them.

Paul testified to Jews and Greeks of repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ.

He knew that trouble awaited him. The Holy Spirit warned him that bonds and afflictions were ahead. Yet Paul said that none of these things moved him.

Acts 20:24 captures his attitude: he did not count his life dear to himself so that he might finish his course with joy and the ministry he received from the Lord Jesus, to testify of the Gospel of the grace of God.

That is the goal: finish the course with joy.

## Do Not Hold Back What Is Profitable

Paul tells the elders that they will see his face no more. He says that he is pure from the blood of all because he did not shrink from declaring the whole counsel of God.

There is a serious responsibility in ministry. If we hold back what is profitable, we carry responsibility for what we failed to say. But if we faithfully release what God has given for the people's profit, the responsibility for their response rests with them.

Paul then tells the leaders to take heed to themselves and to all the flock over which the Holy Spirit has made them overseers. They are to feed the church of God, which He purchased with His own blood.

The church belongs to Jesus. The flock belongs to Him. Leaders have been entrusted with the responsibility of feeding and overseeing what He owns.

Paul warns that grievous wolves will enter and that even from among the church people will arise speaking perverse things to draw disciples after themselves. Divisions and unhealthy attempts to build personal followings are real dangers.

Therefore, leaders must watch and remember that Paul warned the believers continually, night and day, with tears.

There is nothing wrong with repeating a warning when you care for people. Repetition can be an expression of love when the purpose is to protect the flock.

## The Word of Grace Builds Us Up

Acts 20:32 says, "And now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified."

Paul did not covet anyone's silver, gold, or clothing. He did not look at someone's possessions and desire to have them. He worked with his own hands.

His ministry was not driven by personal gain. He was concerned about the profit of the people and the fulfillment of the calling he had received.

After he had said these things, Paul knelt down and prayed with them. They wept openly, embraced him, and were especially sorrowful because they would see his face no more.

There was a real relationship between Paul and the churches. He was not remote from them. He wanted to see them face to face.

The Christian life in Scripture is not designed as an isolated existence. The church is a people who see one another, know one another, care for one another, and walk together through different seasons.

## You Are Part of the Body

There is something important about seeing the faces of people and developing real affection for them. Scripture does not present the Christian life as a robot-like experience in which everyone lives as an isolated individual.

You are not the whole church by yourself. You are part of the church. You are one small stone in the larger structure of the body of Christ. You are one piece in something much bigger than yourself.

Jesus has His own plan, His own arrangement, and His own administration in His Word. We must adhere to that arrangement.

It takes humility. It takes merciful service to the Lord through many seasons, changes, disappointments, and challenges.

## Love Truth and Offer Wisdom

We must continue to love truth and desire truth. The church should be a place where wisdom and truth are offered.

God desires that our tongues and voices minister wisdom rather than envy, strife, and division.

The central target remains the same: be like Jesus. Be pure. Be peaceable. Be gentle. Be easy to live with. Be full of mercy. Produce good fruit. Walk humbly with God.

One day we will meet the Lamb Himself as His bride. The seriousness of that future meeting should make us value the mercy we have received and the truth we have been given.

First Timothy 3:15 says that if Paul is delayed, believers should know how to behave in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth.

Notice the phrase: the house of God. It is a house. It is a gathering. It is a place where truth is available. There is an order and an obligation in how we behave in the house of God.

The church is meant to preserve and proclaim truth.

## Conclusion

We should continue to meditate on these things.

God is good. God is loving. God is merciful. The Lamb is pleading now. We should take advantage of this age of mercy and not become casual about it.

We need wisdom. Wisdom is the principal thing. We need the meekness of wisdom. We need to love mercy, walk humbly with God, speak words that build others up, and refuse envy, strife, and division.

We should remain willing to hear truth even when it requires adjustment. The world is filled with lies and shadows. The church must be a place where the truth is available.

We should hold nothing back that is profitable. We should serve the Lord with humility through every season. We should care for the flock that belongs to Him. We should be committed to the Word of His grace, which is able to build us up.

May we be able to offer life, wisdom, and truth as we live on this earth. May we run our race and finish the purpose for which we were created. May none of us leave the earth without accurately arranging and adjusting our lives according to the wisdom of God.

We trust Him and receive these things in Jesus' name. Amen.

- What does it mean to love truth even when truth requires personal adjustment?
- How does James 3 describe the difference between earthly wisdom and wisdom from above?
- What does the sermon teach about meekness and humility?
- How does Paul demonstrate humility and faithfulness in Acts 20?
- What responsibility does the church have as the pillar and ground of truth?

## Reflection

Where has worldly thinking influenced my ideas about success, honor, power, or rest?

Am I willing to receive truth when it corrects me?

Is my speech producing sweet water or bitter water?

Am I growing in purity, peace, gentleness, mercy, and humility?

Am I holding back anything that would be profitable to the people God has placed around me?

Am I learning to find rest in the meek and lowly way of Jesus?
