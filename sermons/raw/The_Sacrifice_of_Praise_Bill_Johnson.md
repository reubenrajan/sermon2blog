# The Sacrifice of Praise — Bill Johnson

I am so glad to be here. It is such an honor to be with the Copelands and so many other friends. I am currently taking my first sabbatical, four months off, but I wanted to make an exception and come to this gathering.

I am also engaged to be married. Linda Frankor is here, and we are getting married very soon. We are keeping the date quiet for now.

Before we get serious, let me share a few stories.

A pastor had dinner at a home of a couple in his church. After he left, the wife said to her husband, “I think he stole our spoon.” It bothered her for a whole year. When they had the pastor over again a year later, she asked, “Did you steal our spoon last year?” He said, “No, I put it inside your Bible.”

An elderly couple walked into McDonald’s and ordered one meal. The man returned to the table, unwrapped the hamburger, carefully cut it in half, split the fries evenly, and put two straws into the soda. The woman began to eat while the man watched and took an occasional sip. A young man offered to buy them another meal so they would not have to share. They declined, saying they were used to sharing everything. Finally, the young man asked, “What are you waiting for?” The old man smiled and said, “The teeth.”

## A Ministry Directly to the Lord

In 1971, my father, who was my pastor throughout my growing-up years, taught a series from the book of Ezekiel. He taught about ministry in the inner court to the Lord Himself and ministry in the outer court to people.

That series touched me deeply. When he finished the final message, there was no altar call and no response time. It was simply a profound message about the responsibility and privilege of ministering directly to the Lord. I bowed my head and said, “God, I give You the rest of my life to teach me that one thing.”

Churches around the world gather around a sermon every week, and rightly so. The Word of God is central to everything we are and do. The teaching of the Word is paramount.

But Israel camped around the presence.

I believe the Lord is restoring the church to become a presence-oriented people, a presence-oriented culture where the manifested presence of God is the central theme and supreme value, that which we recognize and honor most.

I do not say this in conflict with our heritage or everything we have learned. We have learned so much, and I do not want to lose any of it. But one of the key things the Lord has spoken to me for many years is John 15:7: “Abide in Me, and My words abide in you. You will ask whatever you desire, and it will be done for you.”

The purpose of life is to learn to collaborate with God so that His Word can filter through our personality and our will and become manifest in the earth. But it takes abiding, and it takes the Word.

Abiding means living in the felt realization of His presence. There has to be an awareness of the person of the Holy Spirit, who is God on earth. It is more than an emotional feeling, although emotions are involved. It is a conscious awareness that God is with us.

A friend of mine said, “If He is as big as He says He is, He should not be that hard to find.”

Learning to recognize God's actual presence and living from that place of presence is the supreme value.

## Hosting the Presence of God

The Holy Spirit lives in every believer, but He does not rest upon every believer.

He can and is willing to rest upon us. I once ran an experiment while living in a small mountain community called Weaverville. My wife and children and I were there for 17 years.

There was an organic food store where I had made good friends. My office was downtown, and I would walk to the post office to get the mail. On my way back, I would stop at the back door of the store.

Before entering, I would stop for a moment and turn my affection toward the Lord. He is such a lover and is drawn to heartfelt affection and adoration. I would become aware of the Spirit of God resting upon me, then I would enter the store and buy whatever I needed for lunch.

One day the owner called me over and said, “Bill, when you walk into the store, something is different.” I was not the only believer in the shop, and I was not the most spiritual. I may have simply been the only one who paused long enough to become aware of the Spirit and then walk into the environment hosting the King of Glory and the Holy Spirit.

There is something that happens when we give Him place to manifest His presence and glory.

We read about people laying sick people in the street so that Peter’s shadow might pass over them. Your shadow will always release whatever overshadows you.

If worry overshadows you, worry is what your shadow releases. If fear overshadows you, fear is what your shadow releases. If resentment, bitterness, or indifference overshadows you, those are what you carry into the environment.

But if God's presence overshadows you, His manifested presence begins to alter the environment around you.

When you walk into a hospital room where the doctors say there is almost no chance someone will survive the night, the situation is different when you walk through the door carrying someone for whom nothing is impossible.

We must adjust our thinking and our appetites to this God who knows no impossibility.

## Nothing Is Impossible Must Become a Lifestyle

It has to move from the lyrics of songs that declare that nothing is impossible to a way of life built around that conviction.

I actually owe Him a lifestyle of risk.

I have to become inconvenienced and pursue things that look intimidating. It is not enough for my theology to be something I can quote or sing. That is a good beginning, but it must produce a change in my conduct, my behavior, the way I think, and the way I see.

Many of us have experienced supernatural provision. The amazing thing about God is that when He exposes His nature and His works to us, it changes not only who we are but also our responsibility.

Once I have seen supernatural supply, it becomes illegal for me to face a problem based on what I have. Once I have experienced supernatural supply, I no longer have the right to begin a thought process with my limitations.

God is teaching us to have a renewed mind. A renewed mind is not simply the ability to quote a Scripture for a problem. That is a beginning place.

You know your mind is renewed when the impossible looks logical.

You see through a different lens. You see from a divine perspective. That is part of what repentance is. Repentance means changing the way we think.

There is a difference between repenting enough to be forgiven and repenting enough to see the kingdom. The purpose of repentance is to shift our focus from an inferior reality to a superior reality.

When Jesus said, “Seek first the kingdom,” He was not telling us simply to try to go to heaven. He was commanding us to look for the manifestation of the kingdom of God that perfectly answers the challenge in front of us.

## The Kingdom in the Holy Spirit

Jesus said in Matthew 12:28, “If I cast a demon out of you by the Spirit of God, the kingdom of God has come upon you.”

The kingdom of God is not meat or drink, but righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit. The kingdom is in the realm of the Spirit.

So when we seek first the kingdom, we are looking for the manifestation of the King and His kingdom in broken situations.

For someone under torment who needs deliverance, seeking first the kingdom means bringing that person into the liberty that exists in heaven. It means bringing the blessing and abundance of heaven into the situation. It means looking at the affliction or challenge in front of us and asking what heaven's answer is, then making that our pursuit.

## Worship as the Priority

At the core of this for me is the lifestyle of worship.

We now have a worship culture in the church, and that represents tremendous breakthrough and years of labor by pastors, teachers, musicians, and songwriters. What was once controversial has become normal.

But when my father first began teaching us to minister directly to the Lord, it was controversial to spend significant periods of time singing to Him rather than only singing about God. It challenged the culture because teaching was often regarded as the main thing.

Every believer has three areas of ministry: to the Lord, to the church, and to the world. That is the order of priority.

The strange thing is that only one of them is urgent: ministry to the world.

Evangelism in its purest form is an overflow of worship.

Isaiah 6 gives a picture of this. Isaiah is in an intense encounter with God when the Lord says, “Who shall I send?” Isaiah volunteers to go.

There are people who are willing to worship but unwilling to go, and that can reveal that they are worshiping worship.

When you catch a glimpse of the Savior and see the One who longs for the lost, it is not either worship or evangelism. It is that He goes with you. His glory and presence go with you into every environment where you have the privilege of bringing people into divine encounter.

We live with this conviction: I owe the world an encounter with God.

The way to ensure that is to stay filled with the Holy Spirit.

I have used the picture of a brand-new bottle of water. Everyone says the bottle is full because it has enough water to sell. But it is not full until it overflows.

The only time you are full is when you are overflowing.

Fullness is not measured by what you contain. It is measured by what you release.

So it is our responsibility to stay filled with the Holy Spirit. I would rather be conscious of Him and have nothing to say than have a lot to say and not be able to find Him.

There is something about awareness of the Spirit of God who is with us and upon us. He makes up for our weaknesses and what we cannot figure out.

## The Gates of Praise

Isaiah 62:10 says, “Go through, go through the gates. Prepare the way for the people. Build up, build up the highway. Take out the stones. Lift up a banner for the people.”

A highway is a place of easy access. What does this mean?

Isaiah 60:18 says, “Your walls will be called salvation and your gates praise.” So the context is the gates of praise.

Go through the gates of praise. Prepare the way for the people.

As we operate in a heavenly realm of encounter with God, we can remove obstacles and offenses in the hearts and minds of people and build a highway for access.

This is a picture of a worshiping community that knows what it means to minister to the Lord. There is something that happens in the unseen realm.

We saw this for years in Weaverville. The town had a reputation for cultic practices and other spiritual confusion. But after years of pursuing God's glory and presence, and doing ministry out of that presence, people who did not know the Lord began to witness what God was doing in the city.

Some people began to consider the Lord because their neighbors carried the presence of God. That does not remove the need to preach the Word. The Word must be spoken. But people can also encounter the reality of God through those who host His presence.

There can be a shift in atmospheres over homes, neighborhoods, cities, and potentially nations through a people who become a worshiping community.

David's tabernacle is a picture of this. It was a continuous, 24/7 expression of worship to the Lord. It was also connected with bringing the Gentiles into the faith.

There is a connection between giving ourselves to God in abandonment and worship and the effect that has on the hearts and minds of people around us.

## Love for One Another

Ministry to the Lord is first, but it has an effect on the harvest.

Ministry to one another takes second place, even in a time when we urgently need to see people converted, because God has sequence and order.

Jesus said people will know that we are His disciples by our love for one another.

Our devotion, care, and compassion for one another shape the lifestyle, culture, and value system of a community.

There is no unity without diversity. Unity without diversity is uniformity, and that is not what the Lord is looking for.

I love the privilege of being with different groups of people. Wherever Christ is confessed, there are genuine expressions of worship. I do not want to lose what has been gained, but I also want to maintain the significance and beauty of genuine heartfelt worship.

One of the words connected with worship means “to kiss.” It speaks of nearness and tenderness.

Worship sometimes involves music, but it is more than music.

## Worship Begins at Home

When I go to sleep at night, I have developed a habit of turning my affection toward the Lord. I am not there to pray for the nations. I want to go to sleep. I leave that for the morning.

When I go to bed, I turn my affection toward the Lord and try to become conscious of Him resting upon me.

I think we would have better days if we had better nights.

The way to have a better night is to go to sleep in the embrace of the Holy Spirit.

There is something profound about this relationship where we simply engage with Him, not because we have something to ask, not because we need to sing a song, but because of adoration.

I am a son. He is my Father, and I love Him.

This creates a pattern that we wake up with in the morning. It prepares us for the day.

Many of us have gone to bed worrying about something and then woken up anxious. We have learned to meditate, but we need to change the subject. We need to meditate on the promise of the Lord and the presence of the Lord and feed on the One who is with us.

## Do Not Worship Worship

My concern about a worship culture is not that we should change everything. It is that we must keep the main thing the main thing.

I do not want to fall into worshiping worship.

The music is wonderful. The musicians and singers are gifted and anointed. But it is possible to come into an environment and be incredibly encouraged without ever having a face-to-face encounter with the Lord or saying anything directly to Him.

Years ago, during a Friday night prayer meeting in Weaverville, we would worship, pray, and intercede for the nations. Sometimes there were only a few people. Later, because of a significant outpouring of the Holy Spirit, the place became packed.

On one night I asked each person to take one aspect of God's nature and praise Him for that characteristic for an extended period.

I wanted them to speak out loud, not because they had to shout or attract attention, but because I wanted sacrifice to remain part of what we did. It had to be more than convenience.

The Bible does not say, “Shout to the Lord, all you extroverts, and introverts just think happy thoughts.”

We took time to praise God for His love, faithfulness, purity, righteousness, justice, and other aspects of His character.

Praying, singing, and speaking in tongues are vital. Acts 2 records people hearing the disciples declaring the mighty works of God. Tongues are a language of prayer, intercession, and praise.

But in this exercise, I wanted people to engage another part of our assignment: loving God with all our mind, as well as our heart, soul, and strength.

Every person began by saying everything they knew about the attribute they had chosen. They exhausted their knowledge within a few minutes. Then, when they ran out of things to say, the Spirit of revelation came upon them and showed them things they had never understood before.

Why? They were stewards of what they knew. They became good stewards by giving what they knew away in praise.

They were generous with their words and thoughts and gave them as an offering to the Lord. Then God began to entrust them with more.

## Thanksgiving, Praise, and Worship

This ministry we have to the Lord includes thanksgiving, praise, and worship.

Thanksgiving is our response to the acts of God, to what He does.

Praise is our response to His nature, to who He is.

The Psalms say that Israel was acquainted with the acts of God, but Moses was acquainted with His ways.

It is critical to recognize the hand of God in our lives and express continuous thanksgiving. One of the most important aspects of life is maintaining a thankful heart.

A thankful heart does not get offended. The antidote for offense is to move into thankfulness.

Thanksgiving means acknowledging what God has done: “You provided for me. You are faithful.” But we must move beyond the act of God to honor Him as the Provider.

Whether kneeling, dancing, shouting, raising our hands, singing, or simply speaking to the Lord, everything we do can be an offering.

Sacrifice is a step beyond convenience. It is where we do something that costs us.

## The Cost of Sacrifice

My concern about worship culture is that we can stop paying a price and coast on what we have obtained.

There are measures of God's glory that we have never experienced or seen. Paying a price does not earn His glory, but it does keep us from coasting.

An inheritance is something you receive freely because someone else paid a price for it.

I have a rich inheritance in the Lord. But if I want to leave an inheritance, I have to pay a price to increase what I received for free.

I cannot simply coast on the yes to God of my parents or grandparents. I am a fifth-generation pastor on my father's side and fourth-generation on my mother's side. I cannot simply live on their obedience. There has to be a personal history that I have with God. There has to be a price I pay to extend and expand what they deposited into my life.

A momentum is being created in worship culture, and I celebrate it. But we must make sure that young and old continue to pay the price and do not simply live under the momentum of their parents' victory or their own victory from years ago.

Thanksgiving may be expressed in words, in dance, or in many other ways. Thanksgiving responds to His works. Praise responds to His nature.

Worship responds to His presence.

You never read about the sacrifice of worship in the same way you read about the sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving. In praise and thanksgiving, what we do is the offering. In worship, I am the offering.

## Worship Is Nearness

Why is it rare to have a true worship service? We have good services, good meetings, good songs, and good response times. But not often is there a corporate gathering marked by such tenderness that this nearness takes place.

Music is important. We are involved in writing songs that shape worship culture, and we care about biblical accuracy. But that kind of worship seldom starts in a corporate gathering.

It starts at home.

It does not always involve music. I like to turn music on, but worship is not measured in minutes or by checking boxes. It is measured by impact.

It is this nearness to Him that unravels you for life itself. You come into a place where you walk out carrying the fragrance of the Lord.

## Honoring Someone Else's Encounter

I once had a hunting dog. He was trained to work with another dog, and when the other dog caught the scent of the bird first, my dog would recognize it and “honor the point.”

I think about that when I enter a worship service carrying distracting conversations or burdens in my mind. Sometimes I am not in the mode to worship.

I remember sitting on the front row while worship started and seeing someone I knew who was a fanatical lover of Jesus already deeply engaged with the Lord. I lifted my arms to honor the point because she had caught the fragrance before I did.

In the natural, one dog catches the fragrance and the other eventually does too. When we recognize those who have already locked in, who are already face-to-face with the Lord, honoring what they have experienced is not only respectful. It can also become the first step into our own increased experience of God.

That is part of how we grow. It is how we expand as affectionate lovers of God who can recognize Him in any situation.

## Keep Paying the Price

I recently charged a worship school with the same message I want to bring to you: keep paying the price.

I have been doing this for more than 50 years. Spiritually speaking, I could live off the dividends of my investment for the rest of my life. I could coast into heaven.

I do not want to coast my way into heaven.

There is something about the sacrifice of praise.

We know that God inhabits the praises of His people. When Scripture speaks about gates, it is often speaking about a place of presence.

Psalm 24 says, “Lift up your heads, O you gates, that the King of glory may come in.”

It is always about presence.

The privilege we have to minister to the Lord is about giving Him a place to land. We make an offering and sacrifice of praise.

It is not works. It is the response that says, “After I have seen what He gave for me, how could I give Him anything less than all of me?”

That has to involve my voice, my body, my emotions, my intellect, and everything about me.

## Praise in the Middle of the Challenge

It is easy to give thanks when the things we hoped for come to pass. It is easy to give thanks when we become pregnant after trying for years, when we finally get married, when we receive a promotion, or when we finally buy a house.

We are supposed to give thanks in those moments.

But I have found that when I am thankful in the middle of my biggest challenge, that is when the greatest impact takes place in my own heart.

When my wife died four years ago, my first response was to raise my hands and give God thanks and honor Him as the healer.

He did not stop being the healer when my wife died.

He did not take a vacation from His character or His promises.

Why did it happen? I do not know. But I do not need to know.

I do not need explanations. He owes me nothing. He never has to explain Himself to me. All I need to know is what I should do next.

## The Pearls of Praise

Revelation 21:21 says the twelve gates of the city are made of pearls, each gate a pearl.

How are pearls formed? Through irritation.

How is the gate of praise that attracts God's presence formed in your life and mine? It is not formed only in blessing or abundance. It is formed in loss, in the things we cannot explain, in the moments when we do not understand why something happened.

Those are the moments when praise becomes costly.

I know that in heaven I will look back and give Him glory. But I will never have a chance in eternity to give Him a sacrifice of praise, because there I will have understanding. There will be no tears, no confusion, and none of the brokenness we experience here.

So I want to seize my moment. I want to take the most painful, challenging, and unexplainable moment and give Him an offering in the middle of it.

Then I am giving Him something I will never have the opportunity to give Him in eternity. I only have this opportunity here.

Sacrifice keeps us young, childlike, and on an edge of growth and development where we remain willing to live beyond convenience.

The Holy Spirit is the Comforter, and He loves to comfort me, but He often makes me uncomfortable first.

Sometimes following Jesus takes us into deeply uncomfortable places. That is part of the life of the believer.

## Surrender, Not Striving

The sacrifices we give Him do not come from striving. They come from surrender.

Great faith does not come from striving. It comes from surrender.

So I want to pray a simple prayer:

“Lord, we offer ourselves to You in a fresh way. Expand our capacity to offer You everything. We do so that You would be glorified in the earth.”

Amen.
