# The Sacrifice of Praise

*Study Blog Edition*

**Speaker:** Bill Johnson  
**Source:** [The Sacrifice of Praise | SWBC 2026 - S2 | July 29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_GN6wyMBbdQ)  
**Date:** July 29, 2026

---

## Introduction

Bill Johnson's message centers on worship as more than music or a church service. Worship is a lifestyle of nearness to God, awareness of His presence, surrender, thanksgiving, praise, and costly obedience.

The central call is simple: do not coast on what God has already done. Keep paying the price of a life that stays close to His presence and learns to praise Him even in difficult and unexplained circumstances.

## A Ministry Directly to the Lord

Johnson begins with a lesson he learned from his father in 1971 while his father was teaching from Ezekiel. The teaching distinguished ministry in the inner court to the Lord Himself from ministry in the outer court to people.

That message led Johnson to pray, “God, I give You the rest of my life to teach me that one thing.”

He argues that the church has rightly centered itself on the Word, but Israel also camped around the presence of God. He believes the Lord is restoring the church to become a presence-oriented people, where the manifested presence of God is the central value.

[John 15:7](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.15.7.NKJV) becomes important here: abiding in Christ and allowing His words to abide in us enables us to live in cooperation with God. The goal is not merely to know words about God, but to know the person of the Word.

## Hosting the Presence of God

Johnson describes the Holy Spirit as living in every believer, while emphasizing the biblical language of the Spirit resting upon believers. He describes learning to pause, turn his affection toward God, and become aware of the Spirit's presence before entering everyday environments.

He connects this to the picture of Peter's shadow in Acts. The shadow reflects what overshadows the person. If worry, fear, resentment, or bitterness overshadows us, those things affect the environments we enter. If God's presence overshadows us, His presence can alter the environment.

The goal is to host God's presence so that what we carry can affect other people.

## Nothing Is Impossible Must Become a Lifestyle

Johnson challenges believers to move the truth that “nothing is impossible” from songs and theology into daily conduct.

A renewed mind changes behavior. It produces risk, inconvenience, and a willingness to pursue what God says even when the situation looks intimidating.

He makes a strong connection between supernatural provision and responsibility. Once a person has experienced God providing supernaturally, that person should not begin future problems from a mindset of limitation.

He describes repentance as a change in perspective. The purpose of repentance is not only forgiveness but a shift from an inferior view of reality to a superior one.

## The Kingdom Is Present

[Matthew 12:28](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/MAT.12.28.NKJV) is used to show that the kingdom of God is made manifest through the Spirit of God.

Johnson emphasizes that seeking the kingdom means looking for the manifestation of God's answer to the broken situation in front of us. The kingdom is associated with righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit.

The believer is called to look for heaven's answer and pursue it in the situation at hand.

## Worship as the Priority

Johnson describes three areas of ministry for every believer: ministry to the Lord, ministry to the church, and ministry to the world. He presents this as an order of priority.

Evangelism is described as an overflow of worship. [Isaiah 6](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/ISA.6.NKJV) provides the model: Isaiah encounters God, hears “Who shall I send?”, and responds by volunteering to go.

True worship does not make believers less willing to evangelize. It produces people who carry God's presence into the world.

Johnson summarizes the idea with the conviction: “I owe the world an encounter with God.”

## Fullness Means Overflow

Johnson uses a bottle of water to explain spiritual fullness. A bottle may be full enough for sale, but it is not full in the fullest sense until it overflows.

“Fullness isn't measured by what you contain. It's measured by what you release.”

The implication is that believers should stay filled with the Holy Spirit so that God's presence and life can overflow to others.

## The Gates of Praise

[Isaiah 62:10](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/ISA.62.10.NKJV) says, “Go through, go through the gates. Prepare the way for the people. Build up, build up the highway. Take out the stones. Lift up a banner for the people.”

Johnson connects this with [Isaiah 60:18](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/ISA.60.18.NKJV), where the gates are called praise. He sees the gates of praise as a prophetic picture of preparing a way for people to encounter God.

Praise creates a kind of highway. It removes obstacles and prepares hearts for encounter.

He points to his years in Weaverville, where a community known for spiritual confusion became a worshiping community. Over time, people who did not know the Lord began to notice what God was doing.

The result was not a replacement for preaching the Word. The Word still had to be spoken. But people also encountered God through believers who carried His presence.

## Love for One Another

Ministry to the Lord is first, but it affects the harvest. Ministry to the church is second because Jesus said people would know His disciples by their love for one another.

The way believers care for each other shapes the culture and values of the community.

Johnson also notes that unity without diversity becomes uniformity. He celebrates genuine diversity among groups that confess Christ and encourages the church to retain the beauty of heartfelt worship.

## Worship Begins at Home

Worship is not limited to corporate gatherings. Johnson describes a personal habit of turning his affection toward God as he goes to sleep.

He is not trying to complete a prayer agenda at bedtime. He simply wants to become conscious of the Lord's presence.

He says, “I think we'd have better days if we had better nights.” Going to sleep in awareness of the Holy Spirit prepares a person for the next day.

This practice also gives a practical answer to anxious thoughts. Instead of meditating on fear and tomorrow's problems, a believer can shift attention to God's promise and presence.

## Do Not Worship Worship

Johnson's concern is not with worship music. He praises the progress that has made worship common across many churches.

His concern is that worship itself can become the object of worship.

A person can be encouraged by excellent music and never speak directly to God. A genuine worship culture must remain centered on nearness to Him.

Johnson also argues that genuine worship often starts at home rather than in a corporate gathering. Music can help, but the essence is not the song, the duration, or the program. The essence is nearness.

## Thanksgiving, Praise, and Worship

Johnson distinguishes three related expressions:

- **Thanksgiving** is a response to God's works, what He has done.
- **Praise** is a response to God's nature, who He is.
- **Worship** is a response to God's presence.

A thankful heart is also presented as an antidote to offense. Thanksgiving recognizes God's hand and moves the believer away from bitterness.

Johnson insists that believers should move beyond thanking God for what He does to honoring Him for who He is.

## The Cost of Sacrifice

Sacrifice is more than convenience. It is the point where worship costs us something.

Johnson's concern is that a mature worship culture could become comfortable and simply coast on what previous generations have built.

He uses inheritance as a picture. An inheritance is received freely because another person paid the price. But if we want to leave an inheritance for those who come after us, we must pay a price to increase and extend what we received.

Johnson speaks personally as a fourth- and fifth-generation pastor and rejects the idea of living only from the spiritual momentum of his parents and grandparents. He wants a personal history with God.

## Worship Is Nearness

The deepest expression of worship is not performance. It is nearness.

Johnson uses the meaning of worship associated with a kiss to describe tenderness and closeness. That closeness can unravel a person for life itself and cause the person to carry the fragrance of the Lord.

He does not reject music. He values biblical songwriting and excellence. But music must remain a means rather than the object.

## Honoring Someone Else's Encounter

Johnson tells a story about hunting dogs. A trained dog can recognize when another dog has already caught the scent of a bird and “honor the point.”

He uses the illustration for worship. Sometimes he enters a service distracted or burdened, but then sees someone who is already deeply engaged with Jesus. By honoring what that person has encountered, he finds himself drawn into worship too.

In this sense, someone else's genuine encounter can help awaken our own awareness of God.

## Keep Paying the Price

Johnson's direct charge is: “Keep paying the price.”

After decades of ministry, he says he could live on the spiritual dividends of earlier years. But he does not want to coast into heaven.

The believer is called to continue growing, continue surrendering, and continue seeking God's presence.

The sacrifice of praise is not earned favor. It is a response to what Christ has already given.

## Praise in the Middle of Difficulty

It is easy to give thanks when life goes well. It is much harder to praise God in the middle of a situation that is painful, confusing, or unexplained.

Johnson gives a deeply personal example. When his wife died, his first response was to raise his hands and honor God as the healer.

He explains that her death did not change God's character. God did not stop being the healer. Johnson does not claim to know why the loss happened. Instead, he says he does not need an explanation. He needs to know what to do next.

This becomes a central part of the message: praise becomes most costly, and therefore most significant, when offered in the middle of pain.

## The Pearls of Praise

[Revelation 21:21](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/REV.21.21.NKJV) describes the gates of the city as pearls.

Johnson asks how pearls are formed: through irritation.

He uses that picture to describe the formation of the “gate of praise” in a believer's life. Praise is not formed only in abundance and blessing. It is also formed in loss, disappointment, and things we cannot explain.

In heaven there will be understanding. There will be no tears, confusion, or sorrow. Therefore, this present life gives believers a unique opportunity to offer a sacrifice of praise while they are still in the middle of the struggle.

That is something they will never be able to offer in the same way in eternity.

## Surrender, Not Striving

Johnson closes by emphasizing that sacrifices offered to God do not come from striving. They come from surrender.

Great faith also does not come from striving. It comes from surrender.

The final prayer asks God to expand the believer's capacity to offer everything to Him so that He would be glorified in the earth.

## Study Pointers

- What does it mean to make God's presence the supreme value rather than merely adding worship to a service?
- Where have you allowed worry, fear, bitterness, or indifference to become what overshadows you?
- How can your worship become an overflow that blesses the people around you?
- Are you living from a personal history with God, or only from the spiritual inheritance of previous generations?
- What would it look like to offer God thanksgiving and praise in the middle of an unresolved situation?
- How can you cultivate nearness to God at home, not only in corporate worship?

## Reflection

The sacrifice of praise is not simply praising God when circumstances are favorable. It is choosing to honor His character and presence when circumstances do not make sense.

The challenge of the message is to keep the main thing the main thing: God Himself. Worship must remain centered on nearness to His presence. Thanksgiving must keep the heart free from offense. Praise must continue even when circumstances are painful. And surrender must replace striving.

The invitation is to live beyond convenience, to keep paying the price, and to become a person whose inner life carries the presence of God into every environment.
